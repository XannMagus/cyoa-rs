World: "*Grey water, wet rope, and the soft knock of boats against their moorings.* The harbour of Merewick lies beneath chalk cliffs, its warehouses gathered around a narrow tidal channel. Twice each day, a bronze tide bell sounds from the outer pier to announce safe passage across the submerged sill. This morning its frame stands empty. There are no broken bolts, no drag marks, and no bell beneath the pier—only a strand of island eelgrass caught around the fastening pin.\n\n**The missing bell threatens the harbour’s next departure.** Without its signal, the harbour guild has closed the channel. The ferry cooperative wants passage restored before its stores spoil; the breakwater trust insists that the empty frame be examined before anything is replaced. Their disagreement unfolds through notices, shuttered offices, and the steadily growing queue of loaded carts. The mystery stays intimate: something essential has vanished from a place where every sound carries.\n\nThe playable roster contains four distinct identities: **Ajax, the sailor; Ajax, the mason; Nell, the chartmaker; and Orin, the ferry keeper.** The two people named Ajax remain separate throughout, identified in choices and dialogue as **Ajax the sailor** and **Ajax the mason**. Nell and Orin are separate playable people; any NPC introduced later must have a unique name different from every playable name.\n\nThe route is short: the bell frame, the customs steps, then a crossing to **Wren Island**, scarcely a mile offshore. Clues connect these places: pale mortar dust beneath the frame, a fresh scrape on the customs landing, and an abandoned receipt for transporting an unusually heavy cargo. Each can be investigated quickly, but the falling tide limits the time available to launch.\n\n**A chapter break falls during the crossing**, when the boat passes the breakwater and Merewick’s familiar harbour sounds fade. *Ahead, a single dull note travels across the water.* Beyond that break, the island’s sheltered cove opens onto a disused lime kiln, an old bell foundation, and a tidal storehouse whose lower door will soon disappear beneath the returning sea.\n\nThe island holds the reason for the disappearance as well as the bell. Its abandoned signal station once warned the harbour of a dangerous cross-current, and recent repairs suggest that warning system is being restored without guild approval. Recovering the bell will reopen the harbour; understanding why it was moved may expose a neglected danger on the very route it is meant to protect. The final choice concerns where the bell belongs, what evidence reaches Merewick, and whether safe passage can be restored before dusk."
