{"title": "The Two Ajaxes of Salmyra Bay", "world_description": "The world is a late-Bronze-Age sea of salt, myth and quarrelling city-states, where the gods still lean close enough to be bargained with and heroes are made by whoever tells the story loudest. At its heart lies **Salmyra Bay**, a crescent of dark water ringed by white cliffs and olive terraces, and at the mouth of that bay sits the harbour-city of **Ankyra-on-the-Narrows**, the last safe anchorage before the open sea.\n\n*The harbour smells of pitch, brine and burnt thyme.* Its two moles reach out like arms, and between them hangs a bronze chain that is lowered at dawn and raised at dusk. Stacked wharves, tilting net-lofts and stairways of whitewashed stone climb the hillside to the Acropolis, where the shrine of Poseidon the Earthshaker keeps a flame that has not gone out in nine generations. Ship-sheds line the eastern quay. The western quay belongs to the salt-merchants, wine-sellers and ropewalkers. Below the waterline, in flooded caverns known as the **Drowned Stairs**, smugglers keep what the harbour-masters never learn about.\n\nThe city is divided by a quarrel that sounds like a joke and is not one. Two warlords share the name **Ajax**, and each insists the other stole it. **Ajax the Greater**, called the Tower, commands the hill-garrison and the old fleet: a vast, grave, shield-bearing man whose pride is a fortress with no door. **Ajax the Lesser**, called the Swift, commands the fast pentekonters and the harbour irregulars: a quick, bright, sharp-tongued raider who trusts no oath he did not swear himself. Their supporters wear different colours, chant different verses, and avoid the other's streets. The two share one throne-chair, which is carried between their halls each new moon in a procession that regularly ends in thrown fruit and, once, a drowning.\n\nThe harbour's fate hangs on a single voyage. The **Kassandrid Strait**, a three-week passage across the Wine-Dark Deep, is the only route to the island of **Thessar**, where the last grain stores of the old league are kept. Ankyra's granaries stand half empty, winter storms are gathering early, and the **Grain Oath** binds the city to send a fleet to fetch what is owed. The oracle at the Earthshaker's shrine has spoken in her usual riddle: *\"Two shall steer and one shall land; one shall name the sea and one the sand.\"* No one agrees on what it means, but every faction agrees that both Ajaxes must sail, since neither will trust the other with the grain, the glory or the ships left behind.\n\nThe voyage runs through a chain of strange waters. **The Sleeping Shoals** are pale reefs where becalmed ships hear their own dead calling. **Harpy Rock** is a gull-haunted crag that taxes passing sails in bright objects and secrets. **The Isle of Weeping Cedars** is home to a shipwright-hermit who will build or break any hull for the right story. **The Maelstrom of Karyb** is a slow, patient whirlpool that the old sailors say is a god's open mouth. Beyond them, Thessar waits behind mists and a harbour guarded by its own bitter history with Ankyra.\n\nMany hands are working against a clean voyage. The **Salt Guild**, which profits from scarcity, would rather the fleet fail. The **Brotherhood of the Chain**, the harbour's smugglers, offer secret routes for a price they will not name until it is too late. A **cult of Amphitrite's Daughters** believes the sea should be appeased, not crossed, and sabotages hulls at night. Behind them all, a hungry rival city, **Pelagon**, waits to claim Ankyra's harbour the moment its two heroes fail or fall.\n\nThe central conflict is both external and intimate: whether a city can be saved by two men who cannot share a name, and whether the voyage will teach them, or the people who sail beside them, what the oracle meant. The stakes are a winter of famine, the loss of the harbour's independence, and the question of whose story the sea will remember. *The tide turns at dusk. The chain is rising. The ships are waiting.*"}
