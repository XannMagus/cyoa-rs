# The Harbour Without Its Bell

Greyhaven lies inside a crescent of slate cliffs, where narrow houses lean over tidal stairs and fishing boats sleep beneath patched canvas. The harbour is quiet by custom: engines stop at the breakwater, market stalls close at dusk, and departures wait for the sounding of the bronze harbour bell. *On windless nights, even a dropped mooring chain can be heard across the water.*

Now **the bell is missing**. Its tower remains locked, its mounting bolts are intact, and the bronze has left no scrape upon the stone. Since its disappearance, the tide has arrived seven minutes earlier each day. Boats return with wet ropes tied in unfamiliar knots. At low water, a paved road emerges beyond the breakwater—a road absent from every harbour chart.

Two lighthouses guard Greyhaven. The eastern light stands on solid cliff above the salt gardens; the western light occupies a reef reached only by a causeway at low tide. Their archives record different foundations, different disasters, and different traditions of service. Together, their beams mark the safe channel, but lately each illuminates structures that cannot be seen beneath the other: drowned warehouses, an additional pier, a bell tower standing far out at sea.

The **Harbour Trust** controls the docks and insists that ordinary theft explains the disappearance. Its authority depends upon keeping trade moving, and its survey crews have begun dismantling exposed sections of the mysterious road. The **Sounding Society**, custodians of charts and tide records, demands a closure of the harbour until the old measurements can be understood. Beneath both institutions operates the **Low-Water Exchange**, an illicit market held in abandoned salt cellars. It trades in salvage, concealed shipping records, and objects recovered from places that should still be underwater.

Greyhaven's oldest harbour covenant offers a disturbing explanation. The bell was cast from bronze surrendered by every vessel seeking permanent shelter here. Its daily sounding renewed an agreement with the submerged foundations beneath the bay: safe anchorage in exchange for leaving the drowned district undisturbed. **Recovering the bell may restore that agreement—or reveal what the harbour has been required to conceal.** Sound carries strangely across the exposed road, and every attempt to reproduce the bell's note draws water uphill through the drains.

The adventure supports **three to five distinct playable characters**, created separately. Exactly two members of the entire cast are unrelated lighthouse keepers with different histories; both are playable, and each has the name field exactly **Ajax**, without a surname, station label, or epithet. The two lighthouse archives provide separate historical grounding for them. No additional cast member bears that name.

Exploration follows the tides: cellar doors become accessible, the western causeway disappears, and the drowned road lengthens with each passing day. Evidence can be preserved, sold, suppressed, or shared, changing which factions will grant access to their holdings. The central choice is whether to return the bell to its tower, carry it to the submerged district, or break the covenant entirely. *The harbour still looks peaceful. Its boats are beginning to settle toward something moving beneath them.*
