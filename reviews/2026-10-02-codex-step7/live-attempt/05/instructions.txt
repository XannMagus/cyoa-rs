This coincidence becomes important when messages addressed only to “Ajax” begin arriving at the wrong lighthouse, exposing discrepancies that neither station’s records can explain alone.

For generations, the bronze **Homecoming Bell** hung at the outer end of the breakwater. Its sound carried through fog when neither lighthouse could be seen. It also marked the closing of the tidal sluices beneath the quay, warning boats to clear the channel before the current turned. Twelve years ago, the bell vanished during a night of exceptional low water. The official register records it as lost to the sea; the salvage ledger records payment for its recovery. No recovered bell appears in the harbour inventory.

Now its distinctive three-note peal has returned, sounding from beneath the inlet whenever the tide falls below the lowest step of the old fish market. Neither lighthouse can locate it consistently. Sometimes the sound rises through North Lantern’s disused speaking tube; sometimes Low Lantern’s foundations vibrate as though struck. **At each sounding, one sealed passage beneath the harbour opens—and another floods.** The phenomenon follows the tides closely enough to investigate, but leaves only short windows for exploration.

Beneath Greyhaven lies an older harbour: submerged loading stairs, vaulted salt stores and a network of sluice tunnels cut through the rock. The present breakwater was built over its entrance after a catastrophic flood. Surviving charts disagree about the lower channels, and alterations in the stone suggest that some doors were deliberately concealed. Fresh water runs through parts of the tunnels, bringing the smell of peat from inland marshes; others hold seawater so clear that lantern light reveals mooring rings far below.

Three local institutions have competing claims on what lies underground. The **Sounding Guild**, a cooperative of pilots and salvage crews, wants access to the buried channels and insists that the missing bell remains salvage owed to the harbour. Greyhaven’s unease grows through ordinary details: wet footprints inside locked stores, ropes drawn taut on empty moorings, a tide gauge reading higher than the water beside it. The central mystery is what happened to the bell, why its recovery was concealed, and whether returning it to the breakwater would restore the harbour’s defences or disable something built in its absence.
