The envelope catches beneath your two crooked fingers, and you have to set down the lamp to free it without tearing the paper. Whoever sealed it used no wax. A strip of flour paste holds the flap, still soft from the rain.

Across your kitchen table, Nessa Pike watches you work. Water drips from her auburn curls onto the shoulders of her knitted jersey. Behind her, the window frames the harbour far below: slate roofs, a thin fringe of boats, the breakwater disappearing into evening mist. North Lantern’s revolving machinery ticks through the ceiling, steady enough that you ordinarily cease to hear it.

“Went to the island first,” she says. “He told me to bring it up here. Said if anyone was measuring things behind his back, it would probably be you.”

“Very charitable.”

“He used longer words.” Her silver tooth catches the lamplight, but the smile goes quickly. “Read it.”

You put on the spectacles hanging against your wool waistcoat. Inside is a single sheet, folded around a narrow sliver of paper. The sheet carries three lines in a square, deliberate hand.

**Eleven feet, four inches from the quay datum to the seventh tread.**

**You measured it. Who asked you to remove it?**

**The market stair will be dry tonight.**

For a moment your spectacles seem badly adjusted. You lift them, rub the bridge of your nose, replace them. The figures remain.

You remember kneeling with the brass rule against wet stone. A labourer holds your lamp; his sleeve steams above its chimney. Later, in a warmer room, your supervisor turns the revised drawing toward you and rests one clean fingernail on the blank space. *A harmless correction.* You sign because the calculations governing the quay are unchanged, because the workmen need their wages, because you understand the drawings better than you understand the reasons for them.

Nessa leans forward. “That means something.”

“It’s a measurement I made.”

“How long ago?”

“Twelve years.”

She looks toward the harbour. Her hand closes around the strap of her delivery bag.

The enclosed sliver is tracing paper, brittle at its creases. You hold it flat with your left hand. Seven shallow steps descend beside a wall marked with the old survey hatch for masonry retained in place. At the bottom sits a little rectangle labelled **speaking pipe**. The stair itself bears no title, but you recognise the angle where its landing meets the quay. Your pencil once hesitates there over a crack in the stone; even the hesitation survives in the copied line.

“Where did you get this?” you ask.

“In the Low Lantern supply box. Under the oil receipt.” Nessa pauses. “Yesterday, when I left that receipt, there wasn’t a letter.”

From the passage outside your kitchen comes a low metallic knock.

You both turn.

A second follows, deeper, felt through the table beneath your palm. The lamp flame draws sideways although the window is shut. Then comes the third note, with the thin, wavering tail you remember from fogbound mornings when the Homecoming Bell still hangs above the breakwater.

Nessa stands so abruptly that her chair scrapes the flags.

“That’s it,” she says. “That’s what we heard under the stalls.”

You take the lamp into the passage. Beside the outer door, North Lantern’s disused speaking tube protrudes from the plaster, its brass mouth covered by a hinged cap. A bead of water gathers along the rim. You touch it with your sound fingers, then smell them.

Peat. Cold iron.

“That pipe should be stopped below the headland,” you say.

“Should be?”

You lift the cap. Air moves against your face, damp and surprisingly warm. Far down the tube something scrapes, stops, then scrapes again. Distance means nothing inside a pipe; you cannot tell whether you hear movement beneath your boots or across the inlet.

Nessa comes only as far as the kitchen doorway. “My storeroom’s over that stair. Iven put mortar across the bottom door last winter.” She swallows. “I’ve got the key. But if we go down to the market, we go while there are people about.”

The tube exhales again. Behind you, the lighthouse clock begins striking six. Your evening inspection is due, the market lies a steep twenty minutes downhill, and the copied stair rests beside the lamp’s empty place on your table.
