World: "**Grayhallow** is a small harbour town built into a cleft of dark cliffs, where slate roofs climb in steps above a horseshoe quay and the smell of tar, kelp and woodsmoke never quite leaves the air. For three hundred years its life has been set by one sound: the **tide bell**. It hung in the squat stone tower at the end of the Long Mole, rung not by any hand but by the sea itself, through a float-and-chain mechanism that swung the clapper as the flood tide rose. When the bell spoke, the fishing boats turned for home, the cockle-pickers left the sands, and anyone crossing the old causeway to the island knew they had less than an hour.\n\n*Last night, the bell did not ring.* At dawn the harbourmaster found the tower open to the wind, the iron yoke empty, and the great bronze bell, taller than a man and heavy as a cart, **simply gone**. There are no drag marks on the mole and no wheel ruts on the quay. Only a smear of green weed on the tower steps, and wet footprints that walk *out* to the water's edge and stop.\n\nGrayhallow is a quiet town, and it handles trouble quietly. The **Pilots' Guild** runs the harbour from a whitewashed hall above the fish market. Its members are careful, proud men and women who sell their knowledge of the shoals and have every reason to keep the boats safe and in work. The **Masons' Lodge** built the bell tower and has kept it in repair for generations. Its members are tight-lipped about the old construction marks cut into the tower's foundation stones, and some of those marks match carvings found nowhere else but out on the island. The town's lesser folk all keep their own counsel: cockle-wives, net-menders and a lamplighter who walks the mole at dusk.\n\nOut across the grey water of **the Sound** lies **Holm Ure**, a low green island with a ruined chapel, a dozen sheep, and a lighthouse that has been dark for forty years. It is reached on foot by the **Drowning Causeway**, a cobbled road visible only at low water, or by boat in a crossing of about two hours. The island's last keepers, the **Wardens of the Holm**, were said to have given the bell to Grayhallow in the first place, on the condition that it would \"**answer the sea for as long as the sea asks**.\" Lately, fishermen swear they have seen lantern-light moving in the dead lighthouse at night. Last night, two of them claim, they heard a bell ringing faintly from the island, *out of time with any tide*.\n\nThe stakes are simple and close. In three days comes the **spring tide**, the highest of the year, when the flood runs over the causeway faster than a man can run. Without the bell, the cockle-pickers on the far sands will have no warning, and neither will anyone caught on the causeway. The Pilots want the bell found before panic empties the harbour. The Lodge wants it found before anyone asks too many questions about what lies under the tower. And something on Holm Ure seems to want it **exactly where it is**.\n\nThe journey is short but changes everything. **The first chapter** stays in Grayhallow. It is a mystery of salt-stained ledgers, an empty bell tower, a guild hall full of half-truths, and the hiring or borrowing of a boat. **The chapter break comes mid-crossing**, at the **Skerry of Teeth**, a black reef halfway across the Sound, where a sea-fog rolls in without warning and the harbour vanishes behind. Something there will make plain that the bell did not leave by any ordinary means. **The second chapter** lands on Holm Ure, with its ruined chapel, its dark lighthouse and a tidal cave beneath the cliffs where the old Wardens' secret has waited for someone to come and ask.\n\nA local custom matters to the cast. Forty years ago, the trading brig *Ajax* was lost on the Skerry of Teeth on the same night the lighthouse went dark. Grayhallow families named children born that winter after her, so the name **Ajax** still turns up in more than one household. To avoid mix-ups, townsfolk tell them apart by trade: one Ajax is known on the quay as a **sailor**, and the other in the Lodge yard as a **mason**. They are separate people with separate lives. No one else in the story shares that name. Every other islander, guild member and townsperson has a name of their own.\n\n*The atmosphere is hushed and briny: gulls, fog, cold stone, lamplight on wet cobbles. The pace is brisk. This is a mystery to be solved within a single turning of the tide.*"
