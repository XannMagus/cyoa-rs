Outline: The Bell That Kept the Tide
**Grayhallow** is a small harbour town built into a cleft of dark cliffs, where slate roofs climb in steps above a horseshoe quay and the smell of tar, kelp and woodsmoke never quite leaves the air. For three hundred years its life has been set by one sound: the **tide bell**. It hung in the squat stone tower at the end of the Long Mole, rung not by any hand but by the sea itself, through a float-and-chain mechanism that swung the clapper as the flood tide rose. When the bell spoke, the fishing boats turned for home, the cockle-pickers left the sands, and anyone crossing the old causeway to the island knew they had less than an hour.

*Last night, the bell did not ring.* At dawn the harbourmaster found the tower open to the wind, the iron yoke empty, and the great bronze bell, taller than a man and heavy as a cart, **simply gone**. There are no drag marks on the mole and no wheel ruts on the quay. Only a smear of green weed on the tower steps, and wet footprints that walk *out* to the water's edge and stop.

Grayhallow is a quiet town, and it handles trouble quietly. The **Pilots' Guild** runs the harbour from a whitewashed hall above the fish market. Its members are careful, proud men and women who sell their knowledge of the shoals and have every reason to keep the boats safe and in work. The **Masons' Lodge** built the bell tower and has kept it in repair for generations. Its members are tight-lipped about the old construction marks cut into the tower's foundation stones, and some of those marks match carvings found nowhere else but out on the island. The town's lesser folk all keep their own counsel: cockle-wives, net-menders and a lamplighter who walks the mole at dusk.

Out across the grey water of **the Sound** lies **Holm Ure**, a low green island with a ruined chapel, a dozen sheep, and a lighthouse that has been dark for forty years. It is reached on foot by the **Drowning Causeway**, a cobbled road visible only at low water, or by boat in a crossing of about two hours. The island's last keepers, the **Wardens of the Holm**, were said to have given the bell to Grayhallow in the first place, on the condition that it would "**answer the sea for as long as the sea asks**." Lately, fishermen swear they have seen lantern-light moving in the dead lighthouse at night. Last night, two of them claim, they heard a bell ringing faintly from the island, *out of time with any tide*.

The stakes are simple and close. In three days comes the **spring tide**, the highest of the year, when the flood runs over the causeway faster than a man can run. Without the bell, the cockle-pickers on the far sands will have no warning, and neither will anyone caught on the causeway. The Pilots want the bell found before panic empties the harbour. The Lodge wants it found before anyone asks too many questions about what lies under the tower. And something on Holm Ure seems to want it **exactly where it is**.

The journey is short but changes everything. **The first chapter** stays in Grayhallow. It is a mystery of salt-stained ledgers, an empty bell tower, a guild hall full of half-truths, and the hiring or borrowing of a boat. **The chapter break comes mid-crossing**, at the **Skerry of Teeth**, a black reef halfway across the Sound, where a sea-fog rolls in without warning and the harbour vanishes behind. Something there will make plain that the bell did not leave by any ordinary means. **The second chapter** lands on Holm Ure, with its ruined chapel, its dark lighthouse and a tidal cave beneath the cliffs where the old Wardens' secret has waited for someone to come and ask.

A local custom matters to the cast. Forty years ago, the trading brig *Ajax* was lost on the Skerry of Teeth on the same night the lighthouse went dark. Grayhallow families named children born that winter after her, so the name **Ajax** still turns up in more than one household. To avoid mix-ups, townsfolk tell them apart by trade: one Ajax is known on the quay as a **sailor**, and the other in the Lodge yard as a **mason**. They are separate people with separate lives. No one else in the story shares that name. Every other islander, guild member and townsperson has a name of their own.

*The atmosphere is hushed and briny: gulls, fog, cold stone, lamplight on wet cobbles. The pace is brisk. This is a mystery to be solved within a single turning of the tide.*

Character 1: Ajax (the sailor) — A **weathered man of forty** with a lean, rope-hardened build. His face is wind-burned and creased at the eyes from squinting into glare off the water. He has a short, salt-grizzled dark beard, shaggy black hair going grey at the temples, and pale grey-green eyes. An old **white scar runs through his left eyebrow**. His hands are broad and calloused, and a faded tattoo of a brig under full sail covers his right forearm. He usually wears heavy oiled wool, patched canvas and sea boots, with a knitted cap pushed back on his head. *He smells of tar and cold air.*
Born the winter the *Ajax* went down on the Skerry of Teeth, he has spent his whole life on the water: first as a ship's boy, then as a deckhand on coasting brigs, and now as owner-skipper of a battered little lugger called the *Kittiwake*. Everyone on the quay calls him **Ajax the sailor**, and he has stopped minding it.

He was out on the Sound last night, running late with his nets. He is one of the two men who swear they **heard a bell ringing from Holm Ure**, out of time with any tide. The Pilots' Guild laughed at him. *He knows what he heard.*

He has his own reasons to care. His father was a deckhand on the lost brig and never came home, and the lighthouse that should have warned them went dark that same night. **He wants the bell back, and he wants to know why the light failed.** He also has the one thing the mystery needs: a boat, and the nerve to take it across.
Character 2: Ajax (the mason) — A **broad, heavy-shouldered man of forty** who stands very still and seems carved from the stone he works. His fair hair is cropped close and his beard is neatly trimmed and reddish-blond. He has deep-set **amber-brown eyes** and a nose broken years ago and badly set. Fine grey stone dust always sits in the creases of his knuckles, and the tip of his left little finger is missing. He usually wears a leather work apron over plain linen shirts with rolled sleeves, sturdy moleskin trousers and hobnailed boots, and keeps a **mason's square** tucked through his belt.
Born the same hard winter as his namesake, he was apprenticed to the **Masons' Lodge** at twelve and rose to journeyman, and lately to the Lodge's inner circle. In the Lodge yard he is simply **Ajax the mason**. He is patient, exact and quietly devout about his craft.

Ten years ago he repointed the bell tower's foundations. Down there, under the waterline at low tide, he saw **construction marks**: spirals and a broken circle. He was told never to speak of them. Later he found the same marks cut into the chapel stones on Holm Ure, and the Lodge masters ordered him to forget them.

Now the bell is gone, and the Lodge wants the matter closed before anyone looks too closely at the foundations. *He has kept their secrets all his life.* **This time he is not sure he should.** He reads stone the way a sailor reads water, and he suspects the tower was never meant only to hang a bell.
Character 3: Wren Calloway — A **sharp-featured young woman of nineteen**, small and quick, with a mass of curly copper hair she ties back with whatever string is to hand. Her face is pale and freckled and her eyes are watchful and **dark blue**. One front tooth is chipped. Ink always stains her fingers. She usually wears hand-me-down pilot's jackets too big in the shoulders, a long wool skirt kilted up for scrambling on the quay, and stout laced boots, with a **brass pocket compass** on a cord around her neck.
Wren is the Pilots' Guild's youngest apprentice and its only one not born into a pilot family. Her mother was a cockle-picker drowned on the far sands when Wren was eight, on a night of storm when *no one heard the bell over the wind*. The Guild took Wren in out of guilt, put her to copying tide tables and charts, and never quite let her forget whose charity she lives on.

That clerking has given her something the masters don't suspect. She knows the **salt-stained ledgers** better than anyone. This morning she noticed that **three pages of the bell-tower maintenance log have been razored out**, and the Guild Master told her to keep her mouth shut.

*She has never been good at that.* Wren wants the bell found before the spring tide takes someone else's mother. **She also wants to know who in her own Guild is lying.**
Character 4: Nessa Pryor — A **sturdy, sun-and-wind-browned woman of fifty-five** with a round, deeply lined face and a laugh like a gull's. Her iron-grey hair is braided in a thick rope under a faded headscarf. She has bright black eyes and strong forearms, and her hands are scarred from years of shellfish and cold. She **limps slightly on her right leg**. She usually wears layered woollen skirts hitched up over bare shins or rubber boots, a heavy shawl crossed and knotted at the back, and a **cockle rake and basket** that are rarely far from her.
Nessa is the senior **cockle-wife** of Grayhallow. She leads the women and children who work the far sands below the Drowning Causeway, and for thirty-five years she has timed every one of their lives by the tide bell. She knows the sands, the quicksand runnels and the speed of the flood better than any pilot. *She also knows the old songs her grandmother sang*, songs about the **Wardens of the Holm** and what the bell was promised to.

Without the bell, the spring tide in three days will catch her pickers on the flats, and the Guild has already hinted the women should just stay home and go hungry. Nessa won't have it.

She crossed the causeway as a girl and saw the chapel when there were still Wardens' marks fresh on its walls. **She remembers a promise, a rhyme, and a cave the old folk would not name.** She means to remind Holm Ure of all three.

Halfway down the mole, a thin figure in a navy frock coat is pacing back and forth in front of a rope someone has strung across the stones. **Tobias Fenwick**, the harbourmaster. *Morwenna Slade.* The Master of the Pilots' Guild has come to see what her town is saying.
